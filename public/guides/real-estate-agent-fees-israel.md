# Real estate agent fees in Israel - what's standard

Israeli real-estate agent commissions are negotiable, capped by law, and often misunderstood by foreign buyers and olim. Here's exactly how it works.

## The legal cap

By law, an Israeli real-estate agent can charge **up to 2% + VAT** of the transaction price from each side (buyer and seller). Most agents charge the full 2%, but it is **negotiable** - especially on higher-priced transactions.

For a ₪3M apartment, the maximum legal commission per side is:

- 2% × ₪3,000,000 = ₪60,000
- +17% VAT = **₪70,200**

If both buyer and seller use the same agent (common in Israel), the agent collects from both sides - up to ₪140,400 total. This isn't double-dipping; it's standard practice.

## Who pays?

Both sides typically pay their own side's commission. Unlike the US (where the seller pays both agent commissions), in Israel:

- **Buyer pays the buyer-side agent's fee**
- **Seller pays the seller-side agent's fee**
- If one agent represents both, they collect from both

The fee is paid at **contract signing**, not at closing. Once the מתווך (broker) introduces you to the property, they're entitled to commission whether or not you use them for negotiation.

## When does the agent earn it?

This is the most contested point in Israeli real estate law. By statute (Brokers Law 1996), an agent earns commission when:

1. The agent has a **signed broker's agreement** (הסכם תיווך) with you
2. The agent is the **effective cause** (גורם יעיל) of the transaction
3. The transaction actually closes

If you tour a property with Agent A and then close the deal directly with the seller without telling Agent A - you still owe Agent A commission. The "effective cause" rule means once they introduced you, they're entitled to their fee even if you negotiated alone.

**Common scam attempt**: You see a property listed publicly, contact the seller directly, and the seller's agent claims you owe them buyer-side commission too. This is **not** legal. You only owe commission if you signed an agreement with that specific agent.

> **Tip:** Always read the broker's agreement before signing. Common red-flags:
> - Open-ended duration (any honest agent will agree to 30-90 days)
> - Exclusivity that survives termination
> - Commission owed even if the deal doesn't close

## What's negotiable

In a strong buyer's market (or expensive transaction), agents will discount. Realistic targets:

| Transaction price | Standard commission | Achievable negotiated |
|---|---|---|
| Under ₪2M | 2% + VAT | 1.5-2% + VAT |
| ₪2-4M | 2% + VAT | 1.25-1.75% + VAT |
| ₪4-10M | 2% + VAT | 1-1.5% + VAT |
| Over ₪10M | 2% + VAT | 0.75-1.25% + VAT |

Ways to negotiate:

- **Bring multiple offers.** If you've seen 3 apartments with the same agent, they'd rather close at 1.5% than lose you to a competitor.
- **Use them on both sides.** If you're selling your existing apartment and buying through the same agent, ask for a combined-deal discount.
- **Be honest about your budget.** Agents who know you're stretching to make the deal happen often discount to get it done.
- **Bring a buyer's agreement signed in writing, capped at 1.5%.** Make it the agent's choice to accept or walk away.

## When NOT to use an agent

Some sellers list directly on Yad2/Madlan/BebKey without an agent ("ללא תיווך" - "no broker"). If you find the listing directly and tour without involving any agent, no commission is owed.

If you're a serious buyer with time, this can save you ₪50,000+ on a typical transaction. BebKey's filters let you find no-broker listings - they're typically marked as "manual" or "private" source rather than agent-listed.

The catch: no-broker sellers often price slightly higher (because they keep the savings) and you have no one helping you negotiate, schedule inspections, or chase paperwork. Many olim find it worth paying for an agent's expertise.

## Foreign-buyer-specific tips

If you're not a native Hebrew speaker:

- Use an agent who's **bilingual and writes the broker agreement in English**. Major Tel Aviv / Jerusalem firms (Anglo-Saxon, RE/MAX Olim-focused branches) specialize in this.
- Ask the agent to **email you copies of every Hebrew document with English summaries**. Reputable agents do this routinely.
- Don't sign anything you don't fully understand. If the agent can't explain a clause, walk away.

## What the agent actually does (in Israel)

Some olim buyers are surprised that the Israeli agent's role is **narrower** than what they'd expect from a US realtor:

- ✅ Shows you properties matching your criteria
- ✅ Negotiates price between you and the seller
- ✅ Introduces you to lawyers, mortgage advisors, contractors
- ❌ Does NOT handle the legal paperwork (that's the lawyer's job)
- ❌ Does NOT do inspections (you arrange separately)
- ❌ Does NOT handle the mortgage application
- ❌ Does NOT manage the closing process (lawyer's job)

So the 2% is for matchmaking and negotiation only. Most of the work - and your other ₪40-60K in fees - goes to the lawyer.

## Receipt and paperwork

When you pay the broker, you're entitled to:

- A **kabbalah mas (VAT receipt)** showing the full amount including VAT
- A **certified broker's license number** on the receipt (every legitimate broker has one)
- Clear identification of which property/transaction the commission relates to

If the broker resists giving you a kabbalah, they're avoiding tax. Don't pay until you have one in hand. The receipt is also tax-deductible against future capital gains when you eventually sell the property.

## Special case: no-fee buyer's agent

A small but growing segment of the Israeli market: **flat-fee buyer's representation**. You pay a fixed amount (e.g. ₪15,000-₪30,000) regardless of property price, in exchange for buyer-side representation only. The agent works exclusively for you and gets no commission from the seller side.

This can save substantial money on higher-end transactions (e.g. ₪25K vs ₪150K commission on a ₪7M apartment). The buyer's agent has aligned incentives - they're paid the same whether you pay ₪6M or ₪7M, so they'll push for a better price.

Worth considering for serious buyers above ₪5M.
