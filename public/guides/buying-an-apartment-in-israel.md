# How to buy an apartment in Israel - step-by-step

Buying real estate in Israel is more bureaucratic than most countries, but every step is familiar. Here's the full sequence, from "I'm thinking about it" to "I have the keys", with the typical timing and the gotchas at each stage.

## Step 1 - Get your finances in order (1-2 weeks)

Before you start looking, know exactly what you can afford. In Israel, the mortgage system caps your loan-to-value (LTV) ratio by buyer type:

- **Primary residence** (first home in Israel): up to **75% LTV**
- **Upgrader** (selling your existing apartment to buy a new one): up to **70% LTV**
- **Investor** (you already own another Israeli home): up to **50% LTV**

So if you're an investor looking at a ₪3M apartment, you need ₪1.5M of cash on hand. Plus closing costs of roughly **5-8% of the price** for purchase tax, lawyer fees, real-estate agent commission, and minor fees.

Most banks will give you a **pre-approval** in 2-3 weeks. It's worth doing this *before* you start touring apartments - sellers prefer buyers with confirmed financing.

## Step 2 - Find the property (1 week to several months)

When you visit a property, ask for:

- The **nesach tabu** (land registry extract) - proves who legally owns the property
- The **arnona certificate** - shows the municipal tax band and that there are no debts
- Any **planning rights** the apartment carries (e.g. an additional building right that's already approved)

If you can't read these yourself, your lawyer will - but having them in hand before signing saves weeks.

## Step 3 - Negotiate (a few days to a few weeks)

In Israel, the asking price is usually 5-15% above what the seller will actually accept. Make an offer in writing through the agent or directly. Once verbal agreement is reached, the seller's lawyer drafts a **זיכרון דברים** ("memorandum of understanding") or skips straight to the full purchase contract.

> **Tip:** Never sign a "זיכרון דברים" without a lawyer present. In Israel it's legally binding - once you sign, backing out costs you the deposit (typically 5-10% of the price).

## Step 4 - Engage a lawyer (immediately on offer acceptance)

Israeli real estate transactions REQUIRE a lawyer - there's no escrow agent system like in the US. Each side has their own lawyer; the buyer's lawyer protects your interests.

Typical legal fee is **0.5-1% of the purchase price plus VAT**, minimum ₪5,000-₪10,000. The lawyer:

- Reviews the title chain back at least 7 years
- Checks for liens, mortgages, or third-party rights
- Drafts and negotiates the purchase contract
- Files the purchase tax declaration
- Handles the title transfer at the tabu

## Step 5 - Sign the contract (1-2 weeks after agreement)

At signing, you typically pay **10-15% of the price as a down payment**, with the rest to be paid in installments tied to milestones (e.g. lawyer confirms title is clear, building handover, key transfer).

The contract specifies:

- Total price and currency
- Payment schedule
- Delivery date (the day you get the keys)
- Penalties for either side breaching
- What's included (appliances, parking, storage)

## Step 6 - Pay Mas Rechisha (within 60 days of signing)

Mas Rechisha is Israel's purchase tax. See our dedicated [Mas Rechisha guide](/guides/mas-rechisha-purchase-tax) for the brackets. Briefly:

- **First-time primary residence**: 0% up to ₪1.978M, then sliding scale
- **Olim** (within 7 years of aliyah): special reduced bracket
- **Investors**: starts at **8%** from the first shekel

Your lawyer files the tax declaration and you pay directly to the Tax Authority. Late payment penalties are steep.

## Step 7 - Get your mortgage finalized (4-8 weeks after signing)

After the contract is signed and you have a property, the bank does its own appraisal and finalizes the loan. The bank registers a **mortgage charge** (משכון) on the property at the tabu - this is what prevents the seller from selling the same apartment to someone else, and what gives the bank security.

If the bank's appraisal is lower than the purchase price (it sometimes is), you'll need to make up the gap from your own cash. This is rare but worth being prepared for.

## Step 8 - Key handover (the delivery date)

On the agreed delivery date, you pay the final balance via your lawyer, the seller hands over the keys, and the lawyer transfers the title at the tabu within a few weeks.

**Before you sign the protocol of handover**, walk through the apartment carefully. Check:

- All appliances mentioned in the contract are present and working
- No damage that wasn't there at the showing
- Utility meters read and recorded
- All keys (apartment, parking, storage, mail)

Once you sign the protocol, the seller's obligations end. Don't sign it on a Friday afternoon when you're tired.

## Step 9 - Post-purchase logistics (first month)

- Open accounts with **Hot/Bezeq** (internet/phone), **חברת חשמל** (electricity), **חברת מים** (water)
- Transfer **arnona** to your name at the municipal office
- Register at the **Misrad HaPanim** if it's a new address
- Update your **mortgage standing order** at the bank

## Total timing - typical end-to-end

From "I'm thinking about it" to "I have the keys", expect:

- **3-6 months** if you know what you want
- **6-12 months** if you're searching broadly across multiple cities
- **12-18 months** for new construction (most contracts have an 18-month delivery window)

## What you'll spend beyond the price

For a ₪3M apartment as a first-time buyer:

| Item | Cost |
|---|---|
| Mas Rechisha (purchase tax) | ~₪40,000 |
| Lawyer fee | ~₪20,000 |
| Bank fees (appraisal, mortgage setup) | ~₪5,000 |
| Real estate agent (if used) | up to 2% + VAT ≈ ₪70,000 |
| Moving + minor renovations | ₪10,000-₪50,000+ |

Roughly **5-8% on top of the price** - budget this from day one.
