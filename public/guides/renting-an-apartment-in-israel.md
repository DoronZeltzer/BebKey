# Renting an apartment in Israel - the basics

The Israeli rental market is more landlord-friendly than most Western countries - there's no rent control, leases default to one year, and tenants typically post substantial guarantees. Here's what to expect and how to protect yourself.

## Standard lease structure

Almost every Israeli apartment is rented on a **standard one-year contract** with an option to renew for additional one-year periods. Key components:

- **Monthly rent** - quoted in shekels (₪) per month, paid in advance via post-dated checks or bank transfers
- **Security deposit** - typically 1-3 months of rent, held against damages and unpaid rent
- **Guarantor** - either a personal **ערב** (signing co-responsibility) or a **bank guarantee** (ערבות בנקאית, see below)
- **Standing orders** - for arnona, va'ad bayit (building maintenance), utilities
- **Photos at move-in** - a detailed inventory of the apartment's condition

## ערב vs ערבות בנקאית - the two guarantee types

This is the part that confuses foreigners most. Israeli landlords almost always demand **one of two** financial guarantees beyond the security deposit:

### ערב (Arev / Personal Guarantor)

A friend or family member signs a one-page form agreeing to pay your rent and damages if you default. The arev needs to be:

- An Israeli citizen
- Employed (gainfully, with a salary)
- Sometimes: a homeowner

The arev's main risk: if you skip a rent payment, the landlord can sue them directly. They're effectively co-signing your lease.

**Best for**: Olim/foreigners with Israeli friends or family willing to vouch. Free, no bank involvement.

### ערבות בנקאית (Bank Guarantee)

You deposit a sum of money at your bank (typically 3-6 months of rent) and the bank issues a formal guarantee to the landlord. If you default, the landlord claims from the bank; the bank claims from your deposit.

- Costs: ₪200-₪500 setup fee plus ~1% annual fee on the guaranteed amount
- Your money is **locked at the bank** for the duration of the lease - you can't use it
- Returned to you when the lease ends and the landlord releases the guarantee

**Best for**: People with cash but no local guarantor (most olim, foreign students, professionals new to Israel). The standard option for foreigners.

### How much money is involved?

For a ₪7,000/mo apartment, typical demands are:

- **Security deposit** held by landlord directly: ₪7,000-₪14,000
- **Bank guarantee** held by your bank: ₪21,000-₪42,000

So you're locking up ~₪30,000-₪50,000 on top of the first month's rent and broker fee. Cash flow planning matters.

## Real-estate broker (mediator) fees for renters

In Israel, **renters pay broker fees too**, capped by law at **one month's rent + VAT** per side. So a ₪7,000 apartment costs you ~₪8,200 in broker fees on top of the deposits.

This is paid at lease signing, separate from rent. You can negotiate down, especially for longer-term leases - sometimes to 50-75% of one month's rent.

## What's typically included in rent

The Israeli norm:

| Item | Usually included? | Who pays |
|---|---|---|
| Apartment use | ✅ | Rent |
| Va'ad bayit (building fees) | ❌ | Tenant |
| Arnona (municipal tax) | ❌ | Tenant (legal default) |
| Water | ❌ | Tenant |
| Electricity | ❌ | Tenant |
| Internet / TV | ❌ | Tenant |
| Major appliances | ✅ usually | Landlord (if included in lease) |
| Furniture | ❌ unless "furnished" | Landlord (if furnished apartment) |
| Building maintenance | ✅ usually | Landlord |

Always **read the lease** for what's included. Some landlords include water in rent (uncommon but happens); some include va'ad bayit (slightly more common in serviced buildings).

## The protected vs unprotected lease distinction

Israeli law distinguishes between:

- **Unprotected lease (שכירות בלתי מוגנת)** - the modern default. Landlord can refuse renewal at year-end. Tenant has no special rights.
- **Protected lease (שכירות מוגנת / דמי מפתח)** - a legacy from pre-1968. Tenants pay "key money" and have nearly hereditary rights. **Almost extinct in 2026** - only ~3% of leases are still protected.

If anyone offers you a "**דירה מוגנת**" (protected apartment), understand it's a very specific legal structure with implications for both sides. Most modern leases are explicitly "**שכירות בלתי מוגנת**".

## Annual rent increases

By law, the landlord can:

- Raise the rent at lease renewal time (typically by 5-15% depending on the market)
- NOT raise rent during a signed lease period

If you sign a one-year lease at ₪7,000/mo and want predictability, **negotiate a two-year lease** with a fixed price (or a small inflation cap, e.g. CPI). Many landlords agree to this for the certainty of a long-term tenant.

## Your rights when the landlord wants you out

After your lease term ends, the landlord can:

- ✅ Refuse to renew (giving you reasonable notice - typically 30-90 days)
- ✅ Renew at a higher rent
- ❌ Evict you mid-lease without cause (very limited grounds - non-payment, illegal use, severe damage)

If the landlord refuses to renew, you have to move out by the lease end date. Failure to vacate triggers eviction proceedings, which take 3-6 months in court but eventually go in the landlord's favor unless you have a strong defense.

## Standard inspection routine

When moving in:

1. **Walk through with the landlord** and document every defect (chipped paint, scratched flooring, missing appliances)
2. **Take dated photos of everything** - especially the kitchen, bathroom, walls
3. **Read water and electric meters**, record the numbers on the lease
4. **Check that all included appliances work** before you sign

This protects you from being charged for pre-existing damage at move-out. Many landlords resist documenting move-in condition - push back firmly. Reasonable landlords accept it.

## Termination - yours and theirs

Mid-lease termination is generally not allowed by either side without cause. If you need to leave early:

- **Negotiate with the landlord** to find a replacement tenant. If they agree, no penalty.
- **Pay the remaining rent** and walk away (legally enforceable, but landlords often settle for less)
- **Job loss / military duty / divorce** - some leases have hardship clauses. Read your lease.

If the landlord terminates without cause mid-lease, you're entitled to:

- Refund of security deposit
- Compensation for moving costs and broker fees
- Sometimes: an apartment hunt subsidy

## Subletting and Airbnb

Most leases **explicitly prohibit subletting**. If you Airbnb your rental apartment without landlord permission, you're in breach of contract - the landlord can terminate immediately.

Some cities (Tel Aviv, Jerusalem) have additional restrictions on short-term rentals through residential leases, regardless of landlord consent.

If you want to sublet legitimately, get written landlord approval first.

## Common red flags

Don't sign a lease that:

- Doesn't specify the exact monthly rent
- Demands the security deposit in cash (always pay by bank transfer with a receipt)
- Has no inventory or move-in inspection list
- Includes an automatic increase clause without a cap (e.g. "rent may increase at landlord's discretion")
- Prohibits guests or visitors
- Demands you pay for major repairs (the landlord pays - only routine maintenance is on you)

## Bottom line

Israeli renting is more bureaucratic than rented housing in most countries - substantial guarantees, broker fees, careful lease reading needed - but the protections are reasonable on both sides. The biggest financial commitment is the **first-month cash outlay** of ~₪30-50K for typical apartments.

Plan for this. Don't assume rent + deposit is the only cost.
