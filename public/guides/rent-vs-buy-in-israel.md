# Should you rent or buy in Israel?

The classic real-estate question, with an Israeli answer that's different from anywhere else. Here's an honest, math-grounded answer for the major Israeli markets - plus the non-financial factors that often matter more than the spreadsheet.

## The rent-vs-buy ratio for Israeli cities

The standard metric is **price-to-rent ratio**: median apartment price divided by annual rent. Below ~16 strongly favors buying; above ~25 strongly favors renting. As of 2026, rough numbers for Israeli cities (using BebKey's own data):

**Every major Israeli city is above 30** - globally that means renting is mathematically cheaper than buying, by a wide margin. Israeli real estate is among the **most expensive in the world relative to rents**.

But - this is just the math. Israelis buy anyway, in large numbers. Why?

## Why Israelis buy despite the math

### 1. Inflation hedge

Shekel inflation has run 3-5% per year for the past 25 years. Real estate has appreciated faster - roughly 7-9% annually nationwide, 10-12% in central Tel Aviv. Owning is a hedge against the shekel losing value to housing.

### 2. Forced savings

Rent disappears each month. Mortgage payments build equity. Over 30 years, that's typically ₪1.5M-₪3M of forced savings on a typical apartment - money you'd otherwise probably not save.

### 3. No equivalent of rent control

Israeli landlords can raise the rent annually with very limited restrictions. A 30-year-old who rents in Tel Aviv at ₪7,000/mo today will likely be paying ₪14,000/mo by age 50 in nominal terms. An owner with a 30-year fixed mortgage pays roughly the same nominal amount forever.

### 4. Multi-generational wealth transfer

In Israel, the cultural norm is that parents help children buy their first apartment, often by selling theirs and downsizing. Owning is the way wealth crosses generations.

### 5. Tama 38 / Pinui-Binui upside

Owners in eligible buildings can see 25-50% capital appreciation from urban-renewal deals at no cost to themselves. Renters get nothing. See our [Tama 38 guide](/guides/tama-38-pinui-binui).

### 6. Mas Shevach exemption

When you sell your primary residence, capital gains tax is **fully waived** (one sale every 18 months). So all that 7-9% annual appreciation is effectively tax-free for owners. Renters who put the equivalent down-payment into stocks pay 25% on the gains.

## Honest break-even - Tel Aviv example

Same buyer, ₪3M Tel Aviv apartment vs. renting an equivalent for ₪8,000/mo:

**Renting scenario:**
- ₪8,000/mo × 12 = ₪96,000/year
- 25-year rent (assuming 3% annual increases): ~₪3.5M total
- ₪750,000 down payment kept invested at 5% real return: grows to ~₪2.5M
- **Net after 25 years: -₪1M cash, but with a ₪2.5M investment portfolio**

**Buying scenario:**
- ₪750,000 down + ₪2.25M mortgage @ 5.5% over 25 years
- Monthly payment: ~₪13,800
- Total interest paid: ~₪1.9M
- Apartment value at year 25 (assuming 7% appreciation): ~₪16M
- Closing costs, arnona, building fees, maintenance: ~₪600,000 over 25 years
- **Net after 25 years: a paid-off ₪16M apartment plus ~₪0 in liquid savings**

Owning wins on net worth (₪16M vs ₪2.5M) but loses on liquidity. The 7% appreciation assumption is the deciding factor - at 4% historical appreciation, owning wins by less. At 10% historical, owning wins by more.

## The non-financial factors

Spreadsheets miss these but they often dominate the decision:

### Renting wins when:

- **You might leave Israel within 5 years.** Transaction costs (Mas Rechisha, lawyer, agent commission, broker on resale) typically eat 8-15% of value - you need significant appreciation just to break even.
- **You're young and changing jobs/cities.** Mobility matters more than equity at 25-30.
- **You can't comfortably afford the down payment + closing.** Stretching for the cash hurts you in other ways (no emergency fund, no investments, no retirement).
- **You want to live somewhere very expensive (central Tel Aviv) but renting puts you in a better apartment than buying would.**

### Buying wins when:

- **You're staying put for 7+ years.** Long-term appreciation overwhelms transaction costs.
- **You have enough cash for 25%+ down without depleting your emergency fund.**
- **You're approaching retirement.** Owning your home reduces your post-retirement living costs dramatically.
- **You qualify for olim, first-home, or special-program benefits** that reduce the effective price by 5-15%.

## The middle path - "rent your dream, own your means"

Many Israeli professionals do this: they rent the apartment they want to live in (e.g. central Tel Aviv 3-bedroom), but buy a smaller apartment in a periphery city (e.g. Ashkelon, Be'er Sheva, Yavne) as an investment property. The investment property earns rental income that covers part of their Tel Aviv rent, and gives them equity exposure.

The math: a ₪1.5M Be'er Sheva apartment at 50% LTV (investor terms) costs ₪750K down. Rents for ~₪3,500/mo. After mortgage payments and arnona, you net ~₪700/mo in cash flow plus ~₪80K/year in appreciation. Effective return on the ₪750K cash: ~12% annual.

This strategy isn't for everyone - being a landlord has hassles - but it's a real third option that most rent-vs-buy articles ignore.

## Bottom line

If you're staying 7+ years and can swing the down payment without overextending: **buy**. Israeli real estate has been a phenomenal long-term investment for the past 25 years, and there's no structural reason to expect that to stop.

If you're staying < 5 years, or unsure: **rent**. The transaction costs alone make short-term ownership a losing proposition.

If you're in the middle: do the math for **your** city, your timeline, and your alternative use of the down payment.
