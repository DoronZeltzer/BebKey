# Tama 38 and Pinui-Binui - Israel's urban renewal explained

Israel has two flagship programs that incentivize developers to renovate or rebuild aging apartment buildings: **Tama 38** (תמ"א 38) and **Pinui-Binui** (פינוי בינוי). Both deliver an upgraded apartment to existing residents at no cost to them, with the developer paying for everything in exchange for new apartments built on top of (or instead of) the original.

If you're buying in a building that's eligible - or already in the process - this affects your price and your future. Here's how each works.

## Tama 38 (תמ"א 38) - earthquake reinforcement

**What it is:** A national outline plan from 2005 that lets developers add new floors to a building in exchange for retrofitting the existing structure against earthquakes.

**Eligibility:** Buildings constructed before 1980 with seismic structural deficiencies. The municipality issues a permit certifying eligibility.

**Two variants:**

### Tama 38/1 - "strengthen and add" (חיזוק)

The original building stays standing. The developer:

- Reinforces the foundation and structure against earthquakes
- Adds an elevator (if there isn't one)
- Builds a **safe room (mamad)** for each existing apartment
- Adds a balcony (~10 m²) to each existing apartment
- Builds 2-3 new floors on top with new apartments to sell

You (the existing apartment owner) get all the upgrades free - including the mamad, balcony, and the elevator - and your apartment area grows by ~13 m² in usable space. The developer's profit comes from selling the new top-floor apartments.

**Timeline:** Once the contract is signed, construction takes 18-24 months. You can keep living in your apartment throughout (with significant noise and disruption).

### Tama 38/2 - "demolish and rebuild" (הריסה ובנייה)

The original building is **demolished** and a brand-new one built in its place. You get:

- A new apartment of your old size + ~25 m² extra, in the new building
- All-new everything - kitchen, bathrooms, windows, AC, parking spot
- Storage room and exclusive balcony
- Significantly higher market value (a 30-year-old building vs. a brand-new one)

You move out for ~24-36 months while construction happens. The developer pays your alternative-housing rent at market rate.

**Caveat:** As of 2025, the original Tama 38 program is being **phased out**. New permits are no longer being granted in most cities - only existing permits in progress can complete. Cities are pushing residents toward Pinui-Binui instead, which has stricter density rules but more predictable outcomes.

## Pinui-Binui (פינוי בינוי) - full neighborhood redevelopment

**What it is:** A larger-scale program where entire blocks (not just one building) get demolished and rebuilt with much higher density. Typical: 5 small old buildings → 1-2 high-rise towers with 5-10× the unit count.

**Eligibility:** A "complex" of buildings designated by the municipality as a Pinui-Binui zone (usually older neighborhoods in central Tel Aviv, Ramat Gan, Bat Yam, Holon, etc.). The municipality publishes a plan; once 67% of owners in the complex agree, the developer can pursue the project.

**What you get** (varies by deal):

- A new apartment of your old size **+30-50%** in the new tower
- All-new construction, much higher quality
- New parking, storage, gym, sometimes pool
- Rental allowance for the 3-4 years of construction
- The developer pays your **moving costs** in and out

**The downside:**

- You move out for 3-4 years (the developer pays your rent at market rate, but it's still disruptive)
- The new tower can be 25-40 floors - significant change to neighborhood character
- The deal can fall through if not enough neighbors sign (need 67% in most variants, 80% in some)

## Buying into a Tama 38 / Pinui-Binui building

This is where it gets interesting for buyers on BebKey. Three possible states:

### 1. The building has a **signed contract** with a developer

Your purchase agreement should reference the developer agreement. You're buying both the existing apartment **and** the right to the future upgraded apartment. Read the developer contract carefully - what apartment do you actually end up with? When is delivery? What if the developer goes bankrupt?

Typical impact on price: **+10-25% premium** vs. an identical apartment in a non-Tama building.

### 2. The building **could be eligible** but no contract yet

The seller might mention Tama 38 "potential" - meaning the building qualifies by age but no developer has come forward. This is speculative; the upgrade may or may not ever happen.

Typical impact on price: **+5-10% premium** for the option value.

### 3. The building has been **rejected** for Tama / Pinui-Binui

Either the structural assessment said the building doesn't need reinforcement (rare), or the city has decided the neighborhood isn't in a renewal zone. The upside is gone - but so is the disruption.

Typical impact on price: **neutral** (slight discount vs. eligible buildings).

## The legal small-print to watch

### "Bituach Chok Mechirat" (חוק מכר דירות)

Israeli law protects buyers from developer bankruptcy by requiring **bank-secured letters of guarantee** for all funds paid. If your Tama / Pinui-Binui deal includes you paying anything to the developer (rare - usually it's free), make sure the law's protections apply.

### Personal guarantees

Some developers ask residents to **personally guarantee** loans the developer takes against the project. **Never sign this.** The developer should bear all risk; that's their business model.

### Bait achzav penalty

The contract should have a hard deadline for delivery, with monetary penalties for the developer if they're late. Standard: ₪50-₪150 per day of delay. If your contract doesn't have this, negotiate it in.

### Renovation rights

If you've already renovated your apartment (new kitchen, redone bathrooms), the developer typically reimburses you a one-time amount for the value of those improvements that will be demolished. Make sure this is documented before you sign.

## Should you buy into a Tama 38 / Pinui-Binui deal?

**Pros:**

- Massive long-term upgrade for free (sometimes +25-50% apartment value over 3-5 years)
- New construction, modern quality
- Often new amenities (elevator, parking, mamad) in old buildings that lacked them

**Cons:**

- Disruption - either living with construction noise (Tama 38/1) or moving out for years (38/2 + Pinui-Binui)
- Risk - the developer goes bankrupt, the project gets delayed indefinitely, neighbors block it
- Tax - the upgraded apartment has a higher market value, so when you eventually sell, capital gains tax (Mas Shevach) may be larger

The math usually works out **strongly positive** if the building is in a strong location (central Tel Aviv, Ramat Gan, etc.) and the developer is reputable. It works out **weakly positive** or neutral in periphery cities where land value increases are slower.

If you're considering it, work with a lawyer who specializes in **urban renewal contracts** (יועץ התחדשות עירונית) - these are dense, technical documents and a generalist real estate lawyer may miss things.
