# Arnona - Israeli municipal property tax

*Arnona* (ארנונה) is the annual municipal property tax in Israel. Every apartment, office and shop pays it. Unlike Mas Rechisha which you pay once on purchase, Arnona is a **recurring monthly or bi-monthly bill** for as long as you occupy the property.

## How it's calculated

Arnona is **per square meter**, not based on the apartment's value. Each city sets its own per-m² rate, and within a city the rate varies by:

- **Use type** - residential, commercial, office, industrial
- **Building age** - newer buildings often pay more
- **Location** - neighborhoods inside the same city have different rates
- **Apartment characteristics** - sometimes balcony / parking get a discount

So a 100 m² apartment in central Tel Aviv might pay ~₪900/month, while the same-sized apartment in Be'er Sheva pays ~₪300/month. Same family, same usage, vastly different bills based purely on where they live.

## Who pays - owner or tenant?

The **occupant** pays arnona, not the owner. So:

- **Owner-occupied home** - the owner pays
- **Rented apartment** - the **tenant** pays, by law

Most lease contracts make this explicit. The previous tenant's name has to be removed from the city's records on the lease start date, and your name added - your landlord helps with this but isn't legally responsible if you fail to register.

> **Tip:** When you sign a lease, always ask for the **last arnona bill** from the previous tenant. It tells you the exact monthly amount and confirms there are no unpaid debts (which can become your problem if the city is sloppy).

## Discounts you may be entitled to

Israel's municipalities are required to offer arnona discounts to certain populations. The percentages vary by city but the categories are universal:

- **New immigrants (olim)** - typically **90% discount** for first 12 months, then **80%** for the next 12. Limited to the first apartment after aliyah.
- **Single parents** - usually 25-50% discount based on income
- **Large families** (5+ children) - usually 20-30%
- **Pensioners on government pension** - typically 25%
- **People with disabilities** (75%+ disability per Bituach Leumi) - typically 80%
- **University students** living away from parental home - typically 25%
- **Low-income households** (תעודת עזרה) - sliding scale up to 90%

**You must apply.** Discounts are not automatic. Visit the city's arnona office with proof (teudat oleh, single-parent certificate, etc.) within the calendar year you're claiming.

## Paying arnona

You'll receive a bill from the city by mail or email. Payment options:

- **Direct debit** (הוראת קבע) from your bank - most convenient, often offered with a small discount
- **Annual prepayment** - usually 2% discount if you pay the full year in January
- **Bi-monthly installments** - the default, no discount
- **Credit card via the city's website** - convenient but no discount

If you don't pay, the city sends reminders, then eventually places a lien on the property. Selling a property with arnona debt is very difficult - your buyer's lawyer will demand it be paid off first.

## Common gotchas

### "I moved in mid-month - am I billed for the full month?"

No. Arnona is **pro-rated by day**. If you move in on the 15th, you owe 16/30ths of that month's arnona, calculated automatically when you register.

### "I moved out but never registered - am I still being billed?"

Yes, until you formally cancel. You can owe months of arnona on an apartment you no longer live in. Always cancel registration when you leave.

### "I'm renting through Airbnb - what arnona class?"

If the property is registered as residential but you're operating a short-term rental business, the city may reclassify it as **commercial** (much higher rate). Several cities (Tel Aviv, Jerusalem, Eilat) have cracked down on this - enforce existing rules. Check before you start.

### "I'm building a new apartment - when does arnona start?"

Arnona is owed from the **occupancy certificate (טופס 4)**, not from when the building is structurally complete. If you take possession but don't move in, you still owe arnona - the city doesn't care if you're occupying or not.

### "The city says I owe arnona for the previous tenant"

You're not liable for someone else's arnona debt - but the bill may be sent to your address if the city's records weren't updated. Bring proof of when you moved in (signed lease + tenant-change form) to the arnona office and they'll correct it. **Don't pay first and argue later** - once you pay you've admitted the debt.

## When you sell - the נסח חתום מסי עירייה

When selling the property, the city issues a certificate confirming all arnona has been paid. This is required for the title transfer at the Tabu - no certificate, no sale. So sellers always settle arnona before closing; your lawyer ensures this happens.
