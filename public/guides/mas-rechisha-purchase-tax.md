# Mas Rechisha - Israeli property purchase tax

*Mas Rechisha* (מס רכישה) is the one-time tax you pay when you buy property in Israel. It's the equivalent of stamp duty in the UK or the property-transfer tax in the US, and it's the largest single closing cost you'll face - sometimes more than your lawyer fee and bank fees combined.

The good news: if this is your only Israeli home, the first ~₪2M of the price is **tax-free**. The bad news: investors pay 8% from the first shekel.

## The 2026 brackets - primary residence

If this is your **only Israeli home** ("דירה יחידה"), you pay a sliding scale. As of 2026, the brackets are roughly:

| Price band | Mas Rechisha rate |
|---|---|
| Up to ₪1,978,745 | **0%** |
| ₪1,978,746 - ₪2,347,040 | 3.5% |
| ₪2,347,041 - ₪6,055,070 | 5% |
| ₪6,055,071 - ₪20,183,565 | 8% |
| Over ₪20,183,565 | 10% |

The bands update January 1st each year for inflation. Always check the current numbers on the Tax Authority site or with your lawyer.

### Example calculation - ₪3M apartment, first home

- 0% on first ₪1,978,745 = ₪0
- 3.5% on next ₪368,295 = ₪12,890
- 5% on remaining ₪652,960 = ₪32,648
- **Total Mas Rechisha: ~₪45,538**

## The investor brackets - second home onwards

If you already own any Israeli home (even partially), the property you're buying counts as an investment property and the rates jump dramatically:

| Price band | Rate |
|---|---|
| Up to ₪6,055,070 | **8%** |
| Over ₪6,055,070 | 10% |

8% from the first shekel. That same ₪3M apartment costs an investor **₪240,000** in Mas Rechisha instead of ₪45,000 - a five-times multiplier.

This is deliberately punitive. Israel taxes investors heavily to discourage non-resident buyers from inflating prices for locals.

## The olim discount

If you made aliyah within the past 7 years, you qualify for a **reduced Mas Rechisha bracket** on your first Israeli home:

| Price band | Olim rate |
|---|---|
| Up to ₪1,978,745 | **0.5%** |
| Over ₪1,978,745 | 5% |

Looks worse than the first-home brackets above, but it's *much* better than the investor brackets if you already own property abroad. For an oleh buying their first Israeli home for ₪3M:

- 0.5% on ₪1,978,745 = ₪9,894
- 5% on ₪1,021,255 = ₪51,063
- **Total: ~₪60,957**

Worse than first-home but much better than the ₪240,000 an investor would pay. The 7-year window is from the **aliyah date** (תעודת עולה), not from when you started living in Israel.

## When does the tax become due?

You have **60 days** from the date you sign the purchase contract to file the **Mas Rechisha declaration** with the Tax Authority and pay the tax. Your lawyer does the filing; you wire the money.

If you miss the deadline:

- Interest accrues from day 61
- Late filing penalty up to ~10% of the tax
- The buyer's title transfer at the Tabu is blocked until paid

## Common edge cases

### "But my parents own a small place in Bnei Brak..."

If you have **any ownership share** in an Israeli home as of the purchase date, you're an investor for Mas Rechisha purposes. Even a 5% inherited share counts.

The workaround: sell or gift away the share **before** signing the new purchase. There are anti-evasion rules, so do this transparently with a lawyer.

### "I'm selling my old apartment to buy a new one"

You're an **upgrader** (מחליף דירה). Israel gives you 18 months to sell the old apartment after buying the new one. During that window you pay the **first-home rate** (not investor) - but if you fail to sell within 18 months, you owe the **difference** (Mas Rechisha adjustment) plus interest.

If you sell the old apartment **before** buying the new one, no issue: you're a first-home buyer when you sign the new contract.

### "I'm buying jointly with my spouse"

Israeli tax law treats married couples as a single unit for Mas Rechisha. You can't split a property purchase into "my half = first home, spouse's half = investor." It's all or nothing per couple.

### "I'm buying a vacation property, not living in it"

Doesn't matter where you live - Mas Rechisha is based on **whether you own any other Israeli home**, not on use. A holiday apartment in Eilat or a Tel Aviv investment property both count as "investor" purchases.

### "I'm buying off-plan (חוזה קבלן)"

The tax is calculated and due based on the **purchase agreement signing date**, but the bracket is determined by the **contract price**, not the future delivery value. Some buyers benefit from rising prices over the construction period (they pay tax on yesterday's price, sell at today's price). The Tax Authority is aware of this and there are anti-flipping rules if you sell within 18 months of delivery.

## How to actually pay

1. Your lawyer prepares **Form 7002** (Mas Rechisha declaration) with the price, buyer details, and exemptions claimed.
2. You sign and the lawyer files it within 60 days.
3. The Tax Authority issues a payment slip with a reference number.
4. You wire from your bank to the Tax Authority's account (your lawyer provides the details).
5. You get a tax-paid certificate (אישור מסי שבח ורכישה) - this is what unlocks the title transfer at the Tabu.

If the Tax Authority disputes your declared value (rare for residential apartments, common for unusual properties), they may issue their own valuation and you can object within 30 days.
